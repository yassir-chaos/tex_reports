\section{Overview} % Top level section

RadioLuna intend to deploy a scientific payload (P/L) into lunar orbit, where the goal of the P/L is the detection of the primordial cosmic HI radiation, originally emitted at 1.4GHz and redshifted to the 20-100 MHz frequency range.

The P/L will be hosted on a small spacecraft (S/C) in lunar orbit, and will operate when the S/C is shielded by the moon from both terrestrial and solar radio emission.

In the current phase, Chaos Engine will be tasked with producing a high-level proposal for a payload that would be integrated into the satellite using COTS components where possible, and to evaluate feasibility with respect to mission requirements.
This document will outline the scope of work, deliverables and key dates.

\section {Scope of Work}
\begin{callout_box}{Note}
Chaos Engineers and customer are both aware that to date no COTS antenna operating at the target frequency range has been identified, and agree that the study strategy will include the assessment of existing antennas operating at higher frequencies (typically VHF band) and customer-led discussions with commercial S/C component providers for custom-designed antennas.
\end{callout_box}

In the current phase, Chaos Engine has been tasked with the preliminary, feasibility-level design of the scientific payload (P/L) for a small spacecraft.

The characteristics of the P/L will be boundary constrained (supplied by the customer), although detailed characteristics may be provided and refined during the activity, an initial volume constraint of 30x30x30 cm will be considered.

While the ideal implementation of the P/L would detect radio emissions in the complete 20-100 MHz, this should be treated as a goal, and the tradeoff between frequency range, cost, complexity, etc., should all be considered during the work.

The design principle will be to, where possible, use flight-proven, commercially available components (COTS), including SDR receivers, P/L compute and the antenna. Custom component design should only be considered when there are not feasible COTS candidates.

Chaos Engine is tasked with:
\begin{itemize}
\item{Producing a block-level design of the complete P/L}
\item{Describing possible modifications to the COTs components}
\item{Estimating both the minimum and maximum budget for Mass, Power, Noise and Volume for the P/L}
\item{Identifying potential mitigations for noise, with specific focus on the sub-systems/components that contribute the most to the noise budget}
\item{Providing rough order of magnitude (ROM) assessment of the cost}
\item{Describing the detection chain and how it operates (incl. Frequency range, etc.)}
\item{Providing a ROM of the data volume produced}
\item{Identifying and describing any software (S/W) modifications to the COTS SDR components and provide a ROM cost of implementation}
\end{itemize}

The report will not go into detailed designs of the hardware and software (Schematic, PCB, Software libraries, etc.) as this depends on the final component selection that will come at a later phase in the RadioLuna project.
