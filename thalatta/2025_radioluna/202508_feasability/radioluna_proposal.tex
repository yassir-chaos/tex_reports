\section {Introduction}
\subsection{RadioLuna Objective}
The objective for \textbf{RadioLuna} is to deploy micro-satellites in lunar orbit as a constellation of sub-systems, rather than the traditional paradigm of a monolithic satellite containing all possible sensors.

This is possible now as micro-satellite launch capabilities have improved drastically, along with the commoditisation of advanced sensing capabilities. Further, pushing advanced computation and processing of the signals downstream from the satellite will reduce the complexity of each sensor node.

Through this 'modular' approach, \textbf{RadioLuna} should help to reduce the cost and risk of any one launch, and encourage a robust academic and commercial lunar ecosystem.

The initial payload proposes the use of a \acrfull{sdr} receiver with minimal customization. This payload should
\begin{itemize}
\item{operate as a payload to a small-satellite platform (presumed to be \href{https://www.ohb-italia.it/satellites-missions/}{M3 from OHB Italia});}
\item{be sensitive to signals in the 20MHz-200MHz range;}
\item{fit within a 30x30x30cm volume budget; and}
\item{where possible, be made from \acrfull{cots} components;}
\end{itemize}

This payload is intended to be deployed in lunar orbit and will use the dark-side of the moon during full-moons to filter out terrestrial and solar interference to be able to receive the 21cm cosmic hydrogen signal \autocite{Pritchard_2012}.

\subsection{Report Objective}
The objective of this report is to develop a systems-level design for a CubeSat payload supporting \acrfull{vhf} band communications using a \acrshort{sdr}.

\acrshort{sdr}s are especially suited to this type of mission as they enable in-orbit configuration without greatly impacting mass or volume of the overall payload. In 2008, the \acrfull{esa} defined \acrfull{sdp}s as payloads "consisting of on-board hardware and software technologies to in-flight reconfigure sattelite payloads for multiple different communications scenario"\autocite{angeletti:2008}.
